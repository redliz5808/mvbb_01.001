Ben Lariccia

A native of Youngstown, I now reside in Philadelphia,. Both of these once heavily industrialized cities were final destinations for many southern and central Italians arriving in the 19th and 20th centuries. In 2008, I donated a collection of over 200 pieces of family immigrant correspondence in Italian (1894-2004) to Youngstown State University's Maag Archives.  Currently, I am a contributing writer at La Gazzetta Italiana Newspaper, a monthly publication. My topics have included: local history, immigration, Youngstown area restaurants, and interviews with notable local Italian Americans. I've coauthored two books.

With Joe Tucciarone, I authored two in-depth books that reveal the tortures of immigration from Italy in the 1870s, a decade seldom covered in histories of this time: "Coal War in the Mahoning Valley: The Origin of Greater Youngstown's Italians" and "Italians Swindled to New York: False Promises at the Dawn of Immigration." Both works are extensively footnoted with abundant maps and illustrations.

In "Coal War" we place the initial Italian settlement in Coalburg, a booming coal miners' camp in Hubbard Township. Unwitting Italian were transported there as replacements during the widespread and violent miners' strike of 1873. Sadly, just a few months into the employment of strikebreaking immigrants, Giovanni Chiesa was murdered by a lynch mob in Liberty Township.

In "Swindled to New York" we bring to light some of the abusive practices and economic pressures that drove Italians to leave their native land. Based on primary contemporary documents, our approach was to open this history from the Italian side. Since the focus of our work has been on the decades before the opening of Ellis Island, we believe we've closed a gap in the history of Italian Americans.

In today's immigration crisis I see some of the same problems affecting newcomers as those that impacted my family.
